## Provenance of the Kenney sounds

Kenney (https://kenney.nl) publishes his audio under CC0. Two routes are used, and the two do not
have the same evidence behind them. This section says exactly what was verified.

1. First-party repositories (KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder, Starter-Kit-3D-Platformer,
   Starter-Kit-FPS). Kenney's own READMEs state: "Assets included in this package (2D sprites, 3D
   models and sound effects) are CC0 licensed" (City-Builder, FPS) and "Sound effects (CC0
   licensed)" (3D-Platformer). The code of those repositories is MIT; the assets are CC0.
2. The community mirror https://github.com/Mcamento8/open-game-sfx-index (commit
   34bbe8b525b5ceff3804915ea745532af11f6055), which republishes Kenney's Impact Sounds, Sci-Fi Sounds, RPG Audio, Music
   Jingles, Digital Audio and Interface Sounds packs and OpenGameArt CC0 packs. A mirror is not
   first-party. Verified against Kenney's own repository: exactly one file.
   `Starter-Kit-Racing/audio/impact.ogg` (KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-Racing, commit
   2f2e5f2646dda89cb21d4e8539bab60c6e955dc8) is byte-identical to the mirror's
   `impact-sounds/impactPlate_medium_003.ogg` (both md5 982863bfb97a3b6911abbed44ae53a91). That
   file is not used in the game; it shows that the mirror's Impact Sounds pack holds unmodified
   Kenney files, and nothing more. Every other mirror file the game uses (the Kenney and OpenGameArt
   rows of the table below) rests on the mirror's own record: its `SOURCES.md` and `ATTRIBUTION.md`
   state that each pack page says "License: Creative Commons CC0" (checked by the mirror's author on
   2026-09-21), and `SOURCES.md` lists the official download URLs. Nobody on this project has fetched those pages
   or compared those files with the official downloads yet.

The OpenGameArt pack "Level Up, Power Up, Coin Get" (wobbleboxx, `Upper01.wav` is used) is CC0 on
its OpenGameArt page, as recorded by the mirror; the mirror converted its AIFF files to WAV.

### Closing the gap

On a machine with internet access, download the official packs, unzip them into one folder and run

    python3 assets/audio/build.py --sources <sources> --verify-upstream <folder>

It looks up each mirror file the game uses in that folder, compares md5 (decoded samples for the
converted OpenGameArt file), prints MATCH / DIFFERENT / NOT FOUND per file and exits non-zero unless
all match. `--selftest` exercises it offline on a fake tree. Official downloads (the mirror's
`SOURCES.md`):

   - Kenney Impact Sounds: https://kenney.nl/media/pages/assets/impact-sounds/87b4ddecda-1677589768/kenney_impact-sounds.zip
   - Kenney Sci-Fi Sounds: https://kenney.nl/media/pages/assets/sci-fi-sounds/6b296f9ecf-1677589334/kenney_sci-fi-sounds.zip
   - Kenney RPG Audio: https://kenney.nl/media/pages/assets/rpg-audio/8e99002d76-1677590336/kenney_rpg-audio.zip
   - Kenney Music Jingles: https://kenney.nl/media/pages/assets/music-jingles/f37e530b9e-1677590399/kenney_music-jingles.zip
   - Kenney Interface Sounds: https://kenney.nl/media/pages/assets/interface-sounds/fa43c1dd4d-1677589452/kenney_interface-sounds.zip
   - Level Up, Power Up, Coin Get (13 sounds): https://opengameart.org/sites/default/files/SoundPack01.zip
   - OpenGameArt page of the wobbleboxx pack: https://opengameart.org/content/level-up-power-up-coin-get-13-sounds

tested only on a generated fake tree; it has not been run against the real official downloads.

## Processing

Every file goes through one deterministic pipeline (`assets/audio/build.py`, parameters in
`recipe.json` under `spec` and `kinds`):

1. decode to float mono 44.1 kHz (stereo downmixed, resampled with ffmpeg), apply the per-layer
   ffmpeg filter, slice, and mix the layers;
2. remove DC offset, trim silence below -55 dBFS keeping a 2 ms lead, cap the length per kind
   (ui 1.0 s, work 1.6 s, sting 3.5 s, blast 3.0 s; individual files may be shorter);
3. sounds whose power sits mostly below 150 Hz (at least 50 percent) with under 15 percent in
   150 to 1500 Hz, which laptop and phone speakers cannot reproduce, get a derived "bass body"
   layer added: their own sub-150 Hz band, saturated so it produces odd harmonics, band-limited
   to 180 to 900 Hz and mixed 6 dB under the sound (recipe.json `body`);
4. 1 ms fade-in and a 15 to 40 ms fade-out (longer where a file is cut short);
5. normalise to -18 dBFS RMS measured over 20 ms frames above -50 dBFS, with a -1 dBFS peak
   ceiling enforced by a soft-knee (tanh) limiter, never a hard clip. Gain is raised no further
   than lets the limiter take 6 dB off the peaks, so very spiky sounds (coins, cash till, hammer)
   end up a few dB under -18 dBFS RMS instead of being squashed. The ceiling is tightened and the
   file re-encoded until the decoded MP3 peak is at or below -1 dBFS;
6. encode mono MP3, libmp3lame VBR quality 2 (2 to 38 KB per file, about 620 KB in total).

Per-sound gain and spatial behaviour in game are set in the game's sound catalog, not here.

Reproduce (needs Python 3, numpy and ffmpeg; `<sources>` holds the upstream packs laid out as
the `dir` entries in `recipe.json`):

    python3 assets/audio/build.py --sources <sources> --report
    python3 assets/audio/build.py --sources <sources> --credits
